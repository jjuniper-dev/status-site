02 · Purpose and Scope

This page records the Enterprise Architecture team’s interpreted position on the current Government of Canada AI tool and platform landscape as it relates to Health Canada and PHAC.

Its purpose is to maintain a current working view of the AI environment relevant to departmental planning, architectural framing, governance escalation, and enterprise convergence discussion.

This is not a summary of official strategy documents. It is an internal EA interpretation intended to help distinguish what exists today, what is emerging, what is approved, what is experimental, and what remains unresolved.

04 · Primary Architectural Themes
Control Plane vs Runtime
The most important distinction in the current landscape is between environments that run AI workloads and the governance fabric that makes those workloads defensible at enterprise scale.
  • Runtime enables workloads
  • Control plane governs workloads
  • Both are required for enterprise readiness
  • Operational deployment is not the same as governance completeness
Fragmentation Risk
The primary structural risk: PATH and HAIL are building simultaneously without a defined convergence architecture. Named as a P1 ARB item.
  • PATH (control plane) and HAIL (runtime) advancing in parallel without convergence model
  • Independent experimentation streams compounding ambiguity
  • Weakly aligned governance narratives across initiatives
  • Duplication of controls and unresolved operating ownership
05 · Shadow AI Vectors
Active Shadow Vectors
Specific vectors currently creating governance exposure across HC/PHAC AI activity.
  • CANChat — unclassified scope creep risk; use cases expanding beyond approved boundaries
  • Copilot (M365) — ATO gap unresolved; workforce adoption outpacing formal authorization
  • RADIA — deprecated; references persist in planning documents and may mislead capability assumptions
Governance Exposure
Shadow AI vectors matter because they create invisible control gaps that compound over time.
  • Use cases deployed without ATO or ARB review
  • Deprecated tools referenced as current capability
  • Classification boundaries crossed without formal review
  • Audit traceability absent for untracked workloads
07 · Enterprise Implications for HC / PHAC
ARB Focus
ARB value is highest where it clarifies enterprise boundaries and readiness claims.
  • What is enterprise versus project-specific
  • What is approved versus experimental
  • What is production-ready versus not yet defensible
  • What dependencies must be resolved before scale
Data Governance as AI Control
AI readiness depends not only on infrastructure and models, but also on whether data can be governed, classified, traced, and justified for use.
  • Classification consistency
  • Lineage and traceability
  • Protection and access governance
  • Defensibility for AI-enabled use cases
